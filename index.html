About Me

I am an Applied ML Engineer at Runware in London, where I work on efficient training and inference across a production platform serving 1,000+ models spanning generative media (image, video, audio, and 3D), LLMs, and multimodal foundation models. My work is a continuous cycle of analysing model architectures to locate where compute and memory are spent, and translating ideas from the literature, such as diffusion caching, sparse attention, token routing, quantization, and distillation, into measurable gains on production models, whether applied individually or combined to extract the maximum speed-up a given architecture allows. I am particularly interested in how these methods interact: their gains are often non-additive when combined, so identifying combinations that reduce end-to-end latency without degrading quality requires careful profiling and controlled experimentation on NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell GPUs.

This systems work has led me to a broader research question: how can the spatial, temporal, and physical reasoning capabilities of large multimodal and world models be transferred into models that operate under the constraints of embodied systems? My research interest is therefore efficient world and multimodal models for embodied AI, with a focus on knowledge distillation into compact policies and on training and inference under strict latency, memory, and compute budgets. My independent implementation and adaptation of WPT, using a frozen pretrained OccWorld model on nuScenes in place of the paper's Drive-OccWorld, is the concrete starting point for this work. At Runware I have also implemented and adapted recent efficiency methods beyond their original experimental settings, including HASTE sparse attention for production video models such as Wan 2.2 and LTX 2.3.

Previously, I was a founding engineer at MedTalk, a clinical AI platform used by 50,000+ clinicians, and a Computer Vision Engineer at VisionRD, where I developed industrial perception systems for Toyota and Thal Engineering. I graduated from UET Taxila with a B.Sc. (Hons.) in Software Engineering, a 3.94/4.00 CGPA, and the Silver Medal.

Research Reproductions & Engineering Studies

  • Independent Implementation of WPT: World to Policy Transfer via Online World Model Distillation
    • Independent implementation, August–September 2026. WPT paper | code
    • WPT asks whether the spatial and temporal reasoning of a large driving world model can be transferred into a compact policy that never calls the world model at inference. I implemented the paper on a frozen pretrained OccWorld with nuScenes. The world model rolls out future occupancy, a teacher policy proposes candidate trajectories, and a learned reward model scores them against imitation and simulation signals; policy query distillation and world reward distillation then compress that guidance online into a lightweight student. This is the concrete starting point for my work on distilling world model reasoning into policies that run under strict latency and compute budgets.
  • HASTE: Training Free Video Diffusion Acceleration via Head Wise Adaptive Sparse Attention
    • Runware, May 2026. paper
    • Applied HASTE to Wan 2.2 and LTX 2.3 soon after its May 2026 publication to cut video generation latency without retraining. HASTE is training free and works on top of existing top-p sparse attention such as XAttention and SVG2. Temporal mask reuse skips mask prediction when query and key drift across denoising steps is small, and error guided budgeted calibration assigns per head top-p thresholds by minimising measured output error under a global sparsity budget, where the paper reports up to 1.93× speed up at 720p on Wan 2.1. I traced attention compute and memory, measured sparsity by layer and head, and evaluated the latency and quality tradeoff to identify safe operating points for each production model.
  • Diffusion Inference Caching: FBCache, TeaCache, MagCache, Cache-DiT, Fast3D Cache, and sam3g
    • Runware, 2025–2026. TeaCache | MagCache
    • Diffusion models recompute nearly identical features across neighbouring denoising steps. I implemented and benchmarked the main caching methods [FBCache, TeaCache, MagCache, Cache-DiT, Fast3D Cache, and sam3g] on Runware's image, video, and 3D models across NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell GPUs. For each method I profiled per step CUDA kernel time, peak VRAM, cache hit rates, and output quality metrics (FID, CLIP score, LPIPS) to build a speed versus quality Pareto front per model family, then selected the dominant method for production deployment.
  • Faster Diffusion Fine Tuning with TREAD Token Routing, DreamBooth LoRA, and Context Parallelism
    • Runware, May–July 2026. TREAD
    • Reimplemented TREAD's token routing for LoRA training and evaluated it with DreamBooth style objectives and context parallel execution. Measured convergence, memory use, throughput, and output quality to determine when routing fewer tokens through intermediate transformer blocks produces a useful training tradeoff.
  • Which Optimiser for LoRA Post Training? SOAP, Muon, AdamW, and Lion
    • Runware, May–July 2026. SOAP
    • Following SOAP, Muon, and Beyond: Pushing LLM Pretraining Scales, I ran controlled experiments across optimisers, LoRA rank and alpha, target modules, learning rates, and schedulers for diffusion, LLM, and VLM adapters to find which choices give the best quality and convergence per unit of compute.
  • ConsisLoRA for Content and Style Consistent Style Transfer
    • Runware, May–July 2026. ConsisLoRA
    • Style LoRAs often leak the reference image's content or lose the style. I implemented ConsisLoRA's separate content and style adapters in the training platform so users get style transfer that keeps the subject intact.
  • Segmented Gradient Checkpointing for Flux and MageFlow Transformers
    • Runware, 2026.
    • Standard gradient checkpointing forces a choice between checkpointing every block or every n-th block. I added a mode that checkpoints contiguous chunks of blocks, giving a finer control over memory versus recompute. I evaluated the resulting memory and step latency tradeoff and integrated the option for Flux and MageFlow training.

    • Studied exact context parallel attention through circulating key and value blocks, dynamic sparse patterns for long context prefill, and hardware aligned hierarchical sparsity learned during training. Applied these ideas to multimodal serving with text, image, audio, and video inputs, collective communication, and mixed precision execution using FP8, FP4, INT8, and INT4 where supported by the model and hardware.

  • Training on Base Models and Distilling Turbo Variants
    • Runware, 2025–2026.
    • Few step distilled models tend to commit to global image structure at the earliest sampling stages, while base models form and refine that structure across more timesteps. This creates a representation gap that can make adapters trained on one checkpoint unreliable on the other. I trained on the base model and transferred the result to the distilled variant, selectively freezing layers whose representations diverged most. Preserving the shared layers narrowed the gap and made base LoRAs more useful with distilled models, and distilled LoRAs more useful with base models, while retaining the speed of few step inference.

    • Fine tuned Freepik's EVA based vision transformer for a production moderation taxonomy covering neutral and graded sexual content, suspected minor sexualisation, graphic violence, and extremist content. The model returns per category confidence scores and runs in under one second, making it small enough to evaluate every request. This work used supervised fine tuning, not knowledge distillation.

  • Hardware Aware Attention and Inference Kernel Selection
    • Runware, 2025–2026.
    • Built device capability based selection for NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell, AMD ROCm, and CPU fallback, routing workloads among native PyTorch attention, FlashAttention, SageAttention, cuDNN attention, and architecture specific implementations. Integrated FlashInfer for mixture of experts inference and worked with CUDA, ROCm, CUTLASS, CuTe, and NCCL. Profiling showed that kernel choice is workload and architecture dependent, including cases where SageAttention is faster on Blackwell than FlashAttention 2. The resulting policy avoids unsupported paths and chooses a practical latency and memory tradeoff per model and device.
