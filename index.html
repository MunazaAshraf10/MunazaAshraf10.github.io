About Me

I am an Applied ML Engineer at Runware (London), where I integrate, optimise, and serve 1,000+ production models spanning image and video generation, 3D, speech, LLMs, VLMs, and multimodal pipelines. My day-to-day work sits at the boundary between ML research and systems engineering: I take recent papers—diffusion caching strategies, attention kernel variants, token-routing schemes, context parallelism, knowledge distillation—reproduce them on real hardware (H100, H200, B300, RTX PRO 6000), profile end-to-end through CUDA traces and communication-overhead analysis, and ship whatever actually moves latency, throughput, or memory in production.

That systems-level understanding is what drives my research interest: efficient world models and policy distillation for embodied AI. I am working on how spatial-temporal reasoning learned by large world models can be compressed into compact policies through online distillation, so the policy runs without the world model at deployment. My implementation of WPT with a pretrained OccWorld backbone on nuScenes is the concrete starting point for that line of work. At Runware, I have also applied HASTe (Hierarchical Adaptive Sparse Transformers) to production video models (Wan 2.2, LTX 2.3), systematically profiling attention sparsity, memory, and output quality to extract real speed-ups from the method.

Alongside Runware, I was a founding engineer of MedTalk at Faz Australia, a clinical AI product for real-time transcription, clinical notes, and billing now used by 50,000+ clinicians. Before that I was a Computer Vision Engineer at VisionRD, where I built assembly-line perception systems deployed for Toyota, KIA Motors, and Thal Engineering.

I received my B.Sc. (Hons.) in Software Engineering from the University of Engineering & Technology (UET) Taxila with a CGPA of 3.94/4.0 and the Silver Medal for the second-highest aggregate in my session across all four years.

Research Implementations & Technical Work

  • World-to-Policy Transfer via Online World Model Distillation (WPT) with a Pretrained OccWorld
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Independent research implementation, 2026. WPT paper | code
    • Research implementation of World-to-Policy Transfer using a pretrained OccWorld model for lightweight autonomous driving policy distillation. WPT distils the planning knowledge of a world model into a compact policy network that runs independently at deployment, eliminating the world model from the inference loop entirely. I implemented the full pipeline on nuScenes: a frozen OccWorld teacher that plans over predicted future occupancy grids, a world-reward model that scores planned trajectories, and staged policy and reward distillation into a lightweight student. The build includes 35 unit tests with reproducibility reports, controlled ablation configs, and profiling of the distillation bottleneck—specifically, the communication overhead between the teacher's rollout and the student's gradient updates—to understand where wall-clock time is spent during online distillation.
  • HASTe: Hierarchical Adaptive Sparse Transformers for Video Generation (Wan 2.2 & LTX 2.3)
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026. HASTe paper
    • Applied HASTe's hierarchical adaptive sparse attention to production video models—Wan 2.2 and LTX 2.3—at Runware. HASTe exploits the observation that attention in video diffusion transformers is naturally sparse across spatial and temporal axes, and adaptively prunes low-contribution tokens at each layer. I profiled the method end-to-end on H100 and H200: CUDA kernel traces for attention compute and memory, per-layer sparsity ratios, NCCL communication overhead in multi-GPU configurations, and VRAM high-water marks at each denoising step. The profiling identified which layers tolerate aggressive sparsity without perceptible quality loss and which are quality-critical, enabling a per-model sparsity schedule that delivers real inference speed-ups in production without degrading output fidelity.
  • Diffusion Inference Caching: FBCache, TeaCache, MagCache, Cache-DiT, Fast3D Cache, and sam3g
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2025–2026. TeaCache | MagCache
    • Diffusion models recompute nearly identical features across neighbouring denoising steps. I implemented and benchmarked the main caching methods—FBCache, TeaCache, MagCache, Cache-DiT, Fast3D Cache, and sam3g—on Runware's image, video, and 3D models across H100, H200, and B300. For each method I profiled per-step CUDA kernel time, peak VRAM, cache hit rates, and output-quality metrics (FID, CLIP score, LPIPS) to build a speed–quality Pareto front per model family, then selected the dominant method for production deployment.
  • Faster Diffusion Fine-Tuning with TREAD Token Routing, DreamBooth-LoRA, and Context Parallelism
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026. TREAD
    • TREAD skips most tokens through the middle of the transformer during training without hurting quality. I combined it with DreamBooth-style LoRA fine-tuning and context-parallel training so large image and video models train on more GPUs at once. This is a core part of why the Runware training platform runs about 2× faster than competitors.
  • Which Optimiser for LoRA Post-Training? SOAP, Muon, AdamW, and Lion
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026. SOAP
    • Following SOAP, Muon, and Beyond: Pushing LLM Pretraining Scales, I ran controlled experiments across optimisers, LoRA rank and alpha, target modules, learning rates, and schedulers for diffusion, LLM, and VLM adapters to find which choices give the best quality and convergence per unit of compute.
  • ConsisLoRA for Content- and Style-Consistent Style Transfer
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026. ConsisLoRA
    • Style LoRAs often leak the reference image's content or lose the style. I implemented ConsisLoRA's separate content and style adapters in the training platform so users get style transfer that keeps the subject intact.
  • Segmented Gradient Checkpointing for Flux and MageFlow Transformers
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026.
    • Standard gradient checkpointing forces a choice between checkpointing every block or every n-th block. I added a mode that checkpoints contiguous chunks of blocks, giving a finer control over memory versus recompute, better cache locality, and lower per-step latency, and shipped it for both Flux and MageFlow with documentation in every supported language.

    • Munaza Ashraf
