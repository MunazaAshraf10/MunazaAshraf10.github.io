About Me

I am an Applied ML Engineer at Runware in London. I work at the boundary of machine learning research and systems engineering, integrating, optimising, and serving more than 1,000 production models across image, video, 3D, speech, language, vision language, and multimodal workloads. I reproduce promising research on real systems, study it through kernel traces and communication analysis, and turn the results into measurable gains in latency, throughput, and memory use across NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell GPUs.

My research focuses on efficient world and multimodal models for embodied AI. In particular, I am interested in transferring spatial, temporal, and physical reasoning into compact models through knowledge distillation, while making training and inference practical under strict latency, memory, and compute constraints. My implementation of WPT with a pretrained OccWorld backbone on nuScenes is the concrete starting point for this work: it explores how a capable world model can teach a lightweight policy that no longer needs the world model at deployment. This research is informed by my production work on sparse attention, diffusion caching, quantisation, distributed execution, and hardware aware kernel selection.

Alongside Runware, I was a founding engineer of MedTalk at Faz Australia, a clinical AI product for real-time transcription, clinical notes, and billing now used by 50,000+ clinicians. Before that I was a Computer Vision Engineer at VisionRD, where I built assembly-line perception systems deployed for Toyota and Thal Engineering.

I received my B.Sc. (Hons.) in Software Engineering from the University of Engineering & Technology (UET) Taxila with a CGPA of 3.94/4.0 and the Silver Medal for the second-highest aggregate in my session across all four years.

Research Implementations & Technical Work

  • World-to-Policy Transfer via Online World Model Distillation (WPT) with a Pretrained OccWorld
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Independent research implementation, 2026. WPT paper | code
    • Research implementation of World-to-Policy Transfer using a pretrained OccWorld model for lightweight autonomous driving policy distillation. WPT distils the planning knowledge of a world model into a compact policy network that runs independently at deployment, eliminating the world model from the inference loop entirely. I implemented the full pipeline on nuScenes: a frozen OccWorld teacher that plans over predicted future occupancy grids, a world-reward model that scores planned trajectories, and staged policy and reward distillation into a lightweight student. The build includes 35 unit tests with reproducibility reports, controlled ablation configs, and profiling of the distillation bottleneck—specifically, the communication overhead between the teacher's rollout and the student's gradient updates—to understand where wall-clock time is spent during online distillation.
  • HASTE: Training Free and Adaptive Sparse Attention for Efficient Long Video Generation
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2025. HASTE paper
    • Implemented HASTE soon after its February 2025 publication and evaluated it on production video diffusion workloads. The method combines a training free sparse attention design with an offline profiling stage and online, head adaptive search over hierarchical block sparse patterns. I traced attention compute and memory, measured sparsity by layer and head, and evaluated the latency and quality tradeoff to identify safe operating points for each workload.
  • Diffusion Inference Caching: FBCache, TeaCache, MagCache, Cache-DiT, Fast3D Cache, and sam3g
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2025–2026. TeaCache | MagCache
    • Diffusion models recompute nearly identical features across neighbouring denoising steps. I implemented and benchmarked the main caching methods—FBCache, TeaCache, MagCache, Cache-DiT, Fast3D Cache, and sam3g—on Runware's image, video, and 3D models across NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell GPUs. For each method I profiled per step CUDA kernel time, peak VRAM, cache hit rates, and output-quality metrics (FID, CLIP score, LPIPS) to build a speed–quality Pareto front per model family, then selected the dominant method for production deployment.
  • Faster Diffusion Fine-Tuning with TREAD Token Routing, DreamBooth-LoRA, and Context Parallelism
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, March–July 2026. TREAD
    • TREAD skips most tokens through the middle of the transformer during training without hurting quality. I combined it with DreamBooth-style LoRA fine-tuning and context-parallel training so large image and video models train on more GPUs at once. This is a core part of why the Runware training platform runs about 2× faster than competitors.
  • Which Optimiser for LoRA Post-Training? SOAP, Muon, AdamW, and Lion
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, March–July 2026. SOAP
    • Following SOAP, Muon, and Beyond: Pushing LLM Pretraining Scales, I ran controlled experiments across optimisers, LoRA rank and alpha, target modules, learning rates, and schedulers for diffusion, LLM, and VLM adapters to find which choices give the best quality and convergence per unit of compute.
  • ConsisLoRA for Content- and Style-Consistent Style Transfer
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, March–July 2026. ConsisLoRA
    • Style LoRAs often leak the reference image's content or lose the style. I implemented ConsisLoRA's separate content and style adapters in the training platform so users get style transfer that keeps the subject intact.
  • Segmented Gradient Checkpointing for Flux and MageFlow Transformers
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026.
    • Standard gradient checkpointing forces a choice between checkpointing every block or every n-th block. I added a mode that checkpoints contiguous chunks of blocks, giving a finer control over memory versus recompute, better cache locality, and lower per-step latency, and shipped it for both Flux and MageFlow with documentation in every supported language.
  • Distributed, Sparse, and Multimodal Attention Systems
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Research study and production engineering, 2025–2026. Ring Attention | MInference 1.0 | Native Sparse Attention | vLLM multimodal inputs | NCCL
    • Studied exact context parallel attention through circulating key and value blocks, dynamic sparse patterns for long context prefill, and hardware aligned hierarchical sparsity learned during training. Applied these ideas to multimodal serving with text, image, audio, and video inputs, collective communication, and mixed precision execution using FP8, FP4, INT8, and INT4 where supported by the model and hardware.

    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2025–2026.
