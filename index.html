About Me

I am an Applied ML Engineer at Runware, London, where I integrate, optimise, and serve image, video, audio, 3D, LLM/VLM, and multimodal models on a platform running 1,000+ models in production. My research interests are efficient world and multimodal models for embodied AI: transferring spatial, temporal, and physical reasoning into compact models through knowledge distillation, and making training and inference efficient under strict latency, memory, and compute constraints. That interest grew directly out of my work at Runware, where reproducing new papers on diffusion caching, attention kernels, context parallelism, distillation, and optimisers on real hardware showed me how large the gap between a published method and a model that runs well in production still is, and how much of it comes down to understanding what each layer of a model is doing at each step.

Alongside Runware, I was a founding engineer of MedTalk at Faz Australia, a clinical AI product for real-time transcription, clinical notes, and billing that is now used by 50,000+ clinicians. Before that I was a Computer Vision Engineer at VisionRD, where I built assembly-line perception systems for Toyota, KIA Motors, and Thal Engineering.

I received my B.Sc. (Hons.) in Software Engineering from the University of Engineering & Technology (UET) Taxila, Pakistan, with a CGPA of 3.94/4.0 and the Silver Medal for the second-highest aggregate in my session across all four years.

Research Implementations & Technical Work

  • Diffusion Inference Caching: FBCache, TeaCache, MagCache, Cache-DiT, Fast3D Cache, and sam3g
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2025–2026. TeaCache | MagCache
    • Diffusion models recompute nearly identical features across neighbouring denoising steps. I implemented and compared the main caching methods on Runware's image, video, and 3D models, measuring speed-up against output quality on H100, H200, and B300, and selected the best method per model family for production.
  • Faster Diffusion Fine-Tuning with TREAD Token Routing, DreamBooth-LoRA, and Context Parallelism
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026. TREAD
    • TREAD skips most tokens through the middle of the transformer during training without hurting quality. I combined it with DreamBooth-style LoRA fine-tuning and context-parallel training so large image and video models train on more GPUs at once. This is a core part of why the Runware training platform runs about 2× faster than competitors.
  • Which Optimiser for LoRA Post-Training? SOAP, Muon, AdamW, and Lion
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026. SOAP
    • Following SOAP, Muon, and Beyond: Pushing LLM Pretraining Scales, I ran controlled experiments across optimisers, LoRA rank and alpha, target modules, learning rates, and schedulers for diffusion, LLM, and VLM adapters to find which choices give the best quality and convergence per unit of compute.
  • ConsisLoRA for Content- and Style-Consistent Style Transfer
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026. ConsisLoRA
    • Style LoRAs often leak the reference image's content or lose the style. I implemented ConsisLoRA's separate content and style adapters in the training platform so users get style transfer that keeps the subject intact.
  • Segmented Gradient Checkpointing for Flux and MageFlow Transformers
    • Munaza Ashraf
    • Runware, 2026.
    • Standard gradient checkpointing forces a choice between checkpointing every block or every n-th block. I added a mode that checkpoints contiguous chunks of blocks, giving a finer control over memory versus recompute, better cache locality, and lower per-step latency, and shipped it for both Flux and MageFlow with documentation in every supported language.
