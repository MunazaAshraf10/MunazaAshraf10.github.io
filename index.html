About Me

I am an Applied ML Engineer at Runware in London, where I make generative media (image, video, audio, and 3D), LLMs, and multimodal foundation models fast enough to serve at scale on a platform running 1,000+ models. Every new architecture is a fresh optimisation problem: I profile it to identify where compute and memory are spent, then select and adapt methods from the current literature, including diffusion caching, sparse attention, token routing, quantization, distillation, and whatever else the architecture calls for, to address those bottlenecks. Because these methods interact, and combining them rarely yields the sum of their individual gains, much of my work is designing controlled experiments across data centre and workstation NVIDIA GPUs, from Hopper and Blackwell to Ada generation cards, to find the combination that delivers the largest end-to-end speed-up at the lowest cost in output quality.

This work has led me to a broader research question: how can the spatial, temporal, and physical reasoning of large multimodal and world models be transferred into models that operate under the constraints of embodied systems? My research interest is therefore efficient world and multimodal models for embodied AI, with a focus on knowledge distillation into compact policies under strict latency, memory, and compute budgets. Its concrete starting point is my independent implementation of WPT, adapted to a frozen pretrained OccWorld with nuScenes rather than the paper's Drive-OccWorld. The same instinct to take a method past its published setting runs through my Runware work, where I implemented HASTE sparse attention for production video models such as Wan 2.2 and LTX 2.3.

Previously, I was a founding engineer at MedTalk, a clinical AI platform used by 50,000+ clinicians, and a Computer Vision Engineer at VisionRD, building industrial perception systems for Toyota and Thal Engineering. I graduated from UET Taxila with a B.Sc. (Hons.) in Software Engineering, a 3.94/4.00 CGPA, and the Silver Medal.
